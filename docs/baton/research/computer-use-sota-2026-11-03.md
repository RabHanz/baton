### What this says about Tessaya's hands

1. **The two highest-value ideas in the sources are cheap to take and Tessaya had neither**: read the tree in one cached call (UFO) and
   batch predictable steps with a live-state stop (UFO, Browser Use, Anthropic and OpenAI all converge on this).
2. **Tessaya was missing the action rung the best driver has**: cua acts through the control's own pattern (accessibility route) before any
   pixel; UFO's `set_edit_text` and cua's `invoke_menu` do the same. Tessaya always moved a mouse to the centre of a rectangle.
3. **Tessaya has no occlusion check.** cua binds a pixel click to the capture it came from; Browser Use drops painted-over nodes. Tessaya
   clicked the centre of an element's rectangle without asking what is on top.
4. **Result classes**: cua's `confirmed / suspected_noop` per action is the best recovery design read. Tessaya reports "Clicked" or "Pressed"
   with no evidence (the Ctrl+C no-op found in #52 is the same problem).
5. **Not worth taking**: a second grounding model per click (Agent S3, UI-TARS) costs a call and, in Tessaya's case, a paid or local model;
   reflection after every step (a second model call per step); OmniParser (needs weights and a GPU, licence per detector version).

## What could not be verified

- Playwright's snapshot and ref assignment (lives in the Playwright core repo, not cloned); incremental snapshot diffs are not in
  `playwright-mcp` at this commit.
- Claude in Chrome internals (closed; third-party reverse notes only). OpenAI and Gemini internals (closed).
- Any latency per step for UFO, Agent S3, UI-TARS, OmniParser or cua: no source ran here (they need a model and, for some, a GPU), and none
  publishes per-step time. Vendor benchmark scores are claims.
- Model-planned runs: a CI runner has no model login and the rules forbid an extra paid service, so **every measurement below uses a scripted
  planner**. It measures the tool path and the number of model turns a planner would spend, not whether a model chooses well.
